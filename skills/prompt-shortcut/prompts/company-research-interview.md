I'm preparing for a job interview and want a full interview-prep report.
Before starting any research, check this conversation for the materials
below. If my resume/CV or the job description are missing, stop and ask me
to provide them — don't start the research until I do. For the rest, ask
once and proceed with sensible defaults if I skip them.

Required (ask and wait if missing):
- My resume/CV
- The full Job Description for the role — paste text, attach PDF, or give the URL

Ask, but don't block on:
- My cover letter, if I have one
- Interview stage (HR screening / technical / hiring manager / panel / final round)
- My priorities/constraints (e.g. must be hybrid, target salary, comparing another offer)
- My target roles/career interests, if not already obvious from my resume/cover letter

Once you have the resume and JD, use web search for anything current and do
the following:

1. Company research — overview (what they do, local entity vs global
   parent), their biggest business product(s) and business model, and their
   scale (MNC / large enterprise / SME / startup — employees, revenue,
   market presence).
2. JD-to-resume mapping — a table of each JD requirement against my
   matching experience, flagging real gaps, not just thematic overlap.
3. Alignment assessment — does this role genuinely fit my stated career
   goals/interests, given the JD, the company's direction, and (if
   provided) my priorities/constraints? Give a clear verdict plus caveats
   worth verifying in the interview.
4. Questions to ask interviewers — grouped by theme: role/team specifics,
   tech stack, career trajectory, culture & expectations.
5. (If useful) Likely questions they'll ask me, with STAR-method starting
   points drawn from my resume.

Output: generate the final result as a formatted Word document (.docx) —
cover page, clear headings, tables for structured comparisons, bullet/
numbered lists — not a plain chat response. If I ask for a quick reference
instead, offer a condensed PPTX version.
